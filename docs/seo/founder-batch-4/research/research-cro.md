## Topic 77: How to make website pricing more transparent without undervaluing your service

**1. Founder question and business pain.** "Should we put prices on the site? If we do, we'll look cheap, or competitors will undercut us. If we don't, we get calls from people who can't afford us." Agencies, consultancies, clinics and professional firms lose sales time on unqualified enquiries, and they fear that a single number will anchor buyers on the wrong scope.

**2. Intent and buyer stage.** Commercial investigation, at the "budget and cost" and "choosing a solution" stages. The reader is the owner or marketing lead of a service business deciding on the pricing page.

**3. Queries.** Primary: "should I show prices on my website". Supporting: "should service businesses list prices", "starting at pricing vs contact us", "price range on website", "pricing calculator for services", "how to price custom services on a website", "is a 'from' price legal UK".

**4. Evidence of demand: moderate.** This is a long-running debate in forums and blogs: ukbusinessforums.co.uk/threads/prices-or-no-prices.54886, accountingweb.co.uk/node/189180 ("putting fees on your website"), practiceofthepractice.com/podcast-82-should-i-put-rates-on-my-website, wellbeing.com.au (clinic practices), and pravinkumar.co/blog/why-i-publish-my-pricing-solo-consultant-2026. I couldn't see PAA boxes. Commenters report opposite outcomes (one removed prices and got more work; another listed prices successfully for years), so the article must not promise a direction.

**5. Existing ZSpace pages.**
- `/blogs/saas-pricing-page-optimization` (1,941w) covers SaaS plans, dark patterns and market rules (UK DMCCA/CMA209, India CCPA, AU UTP Act 2026, US FTC). It is SaaS-specific, with self-serve plans, trials and cancellation. Its FAQ "Should we show prices or ask people to contact sales?" is the closest overlap. Keep that answer SaaS-scoped and link to the new page.
- `/blogs/b2b-ecommerce-pricing` (727w) is about product catalogues, contract pricing and quantity breaks. It is not about services.
- `/blogs/subscription-ecommerce-pricing` (990w) is about consumer subscription products.
- `/blogs/fixed-price-vs-time-and-materials` (2,820w) covers contract models and is a natural link for "how we price custom work".
- **Missing everywhere:** a services-business decision on how much price to show. No page has a matrix of display formats, guidance on stating scope (inclusions, exclusions, what moves the price), or the price-display rules that apply to services (UK "from" prices, the UK services-information duty, sector rules such as the SRA's).

**6. SERP weaknesses.**
- jdrgroup.co.uk/blog/why-you-should-show-prices-on-your-website-and-how-to-do-it: opinion, UK B2B, no regulatory angle.
- automatonagency.com/insights/stop-hiding-your-pricing (Apr 2026): agency advocacy for ranges, no decision criteria.
- cubecreative.design/blog/small-business-marketing/should-my-website-list-prices: "many clients won't do business..." is asserted without data.
- wellbeing.com.au/at-home/should-you-display-your-prices-on-your-practice-website...: argues against for clinics, with no reference to health advertising rules.
- sitepoint.com/should-you-post-prices-on-your-website: dated and generic.

Common gaps: they treat the question as a binary yes/no, give no format-by-situation matrix, say nothing on how to describe scope so a number isn't misread, and make unsourced conversion claims. Sheridan's "River Pools cost article" is the usual case study, but the revenue figure attributed to it varies from ~$3M to $25M across sources, so **do not cite any figure** [Inference].

**7. Qualified-buyer potential: high.** Readers own a services website and are about to rebuild or rewrite their pricing or services pages. That fits website development and a CRO audit. Agencies and clinics in India, the UK and Australia match the target markets.

**8. Format and original asset.** A decision guide with a **price-display decision matrix**. Rows: fixed price, published packages, range, "from" price, interactive calculator or estimator, "typical project" examples, and "contact us" only. Columns: when it fits (how standard the scope is, how much variation, buyer type), what the visitor must also see (inclusions, exclusions, price drivers, tax, timescale), the risk of undervaluing and how to mitigate it, and the effect on enquiry handling (qualitative only).

Add a **"scope-transparency block" template**: what's included, what's not, what moves the price, typical timescale, who does the work, and next step. The SRA's mandatory items for solicitors map neatly to this structure (see facts). Present it as a model to borrow, not a legal duty for non-law firms.

Close with a short "rules that may apply" box: UK B2C (CMA209 on "from" prices; the Provision of Services Regulations duty to give the price or the method of calculation), UK SRA for law firms, India Clinical Establishments Rules for clinics (flagged), AU single-price rule (flagged). No conversion-effect numbers.

**9. Service page and CTA.** Primary: `/services/website-development`, with the CTA "Restructure your services and pricing pages". Secondary: `/services/cro-audit`, to check enquiry quality before and after the change.

**10. Recommendation: CREATE. I agree, but change the slug.** I'd use the roadmap-reserved `/blogs/should-you-show-prices-on-your-website` rather than `/blogs/website-pricing-transparency`. It matches the primary founder query word for word, and the roadmap already reserved it. Keep "transparent without undervaluing" in the H1 or title, for example "Should You Show Prices on Your Website? How to Be Transparent Without Undervaluing Your Service". CRO-29 is then fulfilled, not just absorbed. If you prefer the transparency slug, retire the roadmap slug explicitly so it isn't created later.

Guard against cannibalising `saas-pricing-page-optimization`: the new page excludes self-serve SaaS plans, and that page's "show prices or contact sales" FAQ links across.

## Topic 78: Website trust signals — build credibility without overloading pages

**1. Founder question and business pain.** "Visitors don't trust us enough to enquire, so what should go on the site? And how do we avoid cluttering every page with logos and badges?" New firms with no case studies ask a sharper version (roadmap CRO-23): "What can we show honestly before we have proof?"

**2. Intent and buyer stage.** Informational and problem-recognition. The reader is a founder or marketer of a service business, SaaS or a newer brand that is not a Shopify store.

**3. Queries.** Primary: "website trust signals". Supporting: "how to build trust on a website", "website credibility checklist", "trust signals without case studies", "where to put testimonials", "are trust badges worth it", "fake reviews law UK/US".

**4. Evidence of demand: moderate on the SERP, weak in communities.** The SERP has a full page of listicles (below), which shows commercial interest. I found no community threads (Reddit unreachable). CRO-23 in the roadmap is an internal signal of demand.

**5. Existing ZSpace pages.**
- `/blogs/website-trust-and-credibility` (759w, dated 2026-09-24, Web Development category, **no external sources**). It has 13 one-paragraph sections (positioning, design, contact, About, testimonials, case studies, security, policies, content, reviews, brand, author transparency).
  - **Missing:** any evidence base; placement by page type or journey stage, which is the "without overloading" angle; a new-business or no-case-studies section (CRO-23); the legal basis for the fake-review warning, which it frames only as "visitors recognize the pattern"; sector rules (health testimonials); and Google's Who/How/Why guidance.
  - Two FAQs say "visible security indicators reduce hesitation" without a source. Soften these or source them.
- `/blogs/shopify-trust-optimization` (639w) is a Shopify store checklist covering reviews, guarantees, security badges and placement at checkout. **Keep it distinct:** the website page covers services and lead-gen sites and owns the legal "what not to fake" list; the Shopify page links to that list for law and keeps PDP and checkout specifics.
- Related pages: `/blogs/shopify-social-proof`, `/blogs/ecommerce-product-reviews-ux`, `/blogs/marketplace-trust-and-safety`. Link to them; don't duplicate.

**6. SERP weaknesses.**
- capsulink.com/blog/7-online-trust-signals-that-boost-brand-credibility-real-examples/
- compose.ly/content-strategy/trust-signals
- webstacks.com/blog/trust-signals
- oktopost.com/blog/trust-signals/
- devrix.com/tutorial/trust-signals-conversion-psychology/

Common gaps: they say "add more signals", recommending trust bars and badges everywhere, with no placement logic and no warning about clutter. None cite Fogg's or NN/g's research. None cover legal limits on reviews and testimonials (DMCCA, FTC rule, ACCC, AHPRA), and none address businesses with no proof yet.

**7. Qualified-buyer potential: medium-high.** Readers diagnosing low enquiry rates are a good fit for a CRO audit or website rebuild. Clinic and professional-services readers are especially exposed to the sector rules.

**8. Format and original asset.** Update in place to about 2,000 words.
- **Asset 1: trust-signal inventory by page type and stage.** Rows: home, services/product page, pricing, About, case study or portfolio, contact/enquiry form, checkout/booking, post-enquiry confirmation. Columns: the visitor's question at that point, the 1–3 signals that answer it, and where on the page they go. The principle is "match the signal to the doubt at that step", which is how you avoid overloading [Inference, grounded in NN/g's "upfront disclosure" and Fogg's "restraint with promotional content"].
- **Asset 2: "what not to fake" list.** Each item gets a legal reference by market: invented or AI-written reviews; unlabelled incentivised reviews; staff or relatives' reviews without disclosure; cherry-picking or suppressing negatives; bought followers or view counts; "as seen in" logos without real coverage; client logos without permission or a real engagement; certifications that have lapsed; stock photos presented as your team; invented counts ("500+ clients"); fake scarcity or urgency (link to the Batch 2 dark-patterns material).
- **New section "If you don't have case studies yet" (CRO-23):** founder visibility, a transparent process, a scope-transparency block (link to topic 77), real credentials, a pilot or guarantee offer that you can actually honour, content as proof of expertise, and third-party profiles.
- **Health and regulated-professions callout:** in Australia, Ahpra bans testimonials about clinical care in regulated health advertising.

**9. Service page and CTA.** Primary: `/services/cro-audit` ("Get a trust-signal review of your key pages"). Secondary: `/services/website-development` and `/services/ui-ux-design`.

**10. Recommendation: UPDATE `/blogs/website-trust-and-credibility`, absorbing CRO-23 as a section. I agree.** The page is thin and unsourced, and the slug matches the query family. A separate "trust without case studies" page would split a small topic.

Consider moving the category from Web Development to CRO, or keep it if category URLs matter. Retitle to something like "Website Trust Signals: How to Build Credibility Without Overloading Your Pages", keeping the slug. Add a reciprocal link with `/blogs/shopify-trust-optimization`, and make sure that page's FAQ "Can I use fabricated reviews...?" points to the new legal list.

## Topic 79: Ecommerce checkout UX audit — find friction before customers abandon

**1. Founder question and business pain.** "Our checkout loses people and we don't know where or why. What should we check, in what order, and how do we know which fixes are real?" Merchants want a structured review they can run themselves or commission. They don't want another list of best practices.

**2. Intent and buyer stage.** Problem-solving or troubleshooting, close to purchase. The reader is a merchant or ecommerce manager weighing an audit.

**3. Queries.** Primary: "checkout UX audit" / "checkout audit checklist". Supporting: "why customers abandon checkout", "checkout friction", "how to audit checkout flow", "checkout drop-off analysis", "cart abandonment checklist".

**4. Evidence of demand: moderate.** Baymard published a dedicated "How to Audit Your Checkout Flow for Hidden Friction" (13 May 2026), and several vendors publish audit checklists. That points to an established query. I found no community threads.

**5. Existing ZSpace pages, checked in content.**
- `/blogs/why-customers-abandon-checkout` (970w, CRO, 2026-09-28; cro-audit is the first related service). It has a Baymard reasons table, "Not All Abandonment Is Fixable", cause sections, **"A Diagnostic Framework" (an 8-row Cause → Evidence to check table)** and "Testing Checkout Fixes". It is already halfway to an audit.
- `/blogs/ecommerce-checkout-ux` (1,555w, design guide, 2026-09-28). It covers how to design each step (architecture, forms, address, payment, review, errors, progress, mobile, autofill, confirmation, accessibility) and has a "Measuring Checkout" checklist. It is the "how to fix" reference.
- `/blogs/shopify-checkout-audit` (706w, 2026-08-26, **no sources**). It is Shopify-only and generic: completed-checkout rate, guest checkout and "outsized impact", which is unsourced. Its FAQ says checkout.liquid was "deprecated in 2024", which is incomplete. Shopify says checkout.liquid stopped working for the information, shipping and payment pages on 13 Aug 2024. Thank-you and order-status customisations were sunset 28 Aug 2025 for Plus, and script tags on those pages for non-Plus stores on 26 Aug 2026 (see facts). Update that FAQ.
- Also relevant: `/blogs/ecommerce-ab-testing-checkout` (1,335w), `/blogs/mobile-ecommerce-checkout`, `/blogs/global-ecommerce-checkout`, `/blogs/shopify-checkout-optimization`, `/blogs/the-real-cost-of-a-slow-checkout`, and the roadmap's CRO-40 "add to cart but no purchase".
- **Missing:** a step-by-step audit checklist where each item has an **evidence column** (what data confirms or disproves the problem), a severity or priority call, and a method (heuristic walk-through, analytics funnel, recordings, test orders by device and payment method, exit survey, support tickets).

**6. SERP weaknesses.**
- baymard.com/blog/audit-checkout-flow-hidden-friction: strong, but heuristic-only, with no analytics evidence column. It is also Baymard's own funnel into its paid audit and tool.
- baymard.com/learn/checkout-flow-ux-optimization: strong but long.
- convertcart.com/blog/ecommerce-ux-audit-checklist (Nov 2025): vendor checklist covering the whole site, not just checkout.
- aureatelabs.com/guide/ecommerce-ux-audit-checklist/ and designmonks.co/blog/ecommerce-ux-audit: generic and introductory.

Gaps: none pair each checklist item with the evidence that confirms it in GA4, Shopify or recordings. None separate "fix now" from "test first" from "research more". None cover India, UAE or AU payment-method expectations or regional checkout specifics (link to the existing `/blogs/global-ecommerce-checkout`). None warn that the 70% abandonment figure includes browsers.

**7. Qualified-buyer potential: high.** The reader is one step from buying `/services/cro-audit`.

**8. Format and original asset.** A **checkout audit checklist with an evidence column**. Sections follow the flow: cart → account choice → contact and address → delivery → payment → review → errors → confirmation, plus mobile and performance across the flow. Columns: check; why it matters (Baymard reason, where one applies); evidence to confirm (analytics step drop, field errors, payment declines by method, recordings, test order, support tickets); fix, test or research. Add a short "how to run the audit" method (8 steps, about 2 hours for a heuristic pass) and a priority rule [Inference]: severity × how many shoppers reach the step × confidence in the evidence. Link to the prioritisation page rather than repeating it.

**9. Service page and CTA.** Primary: `/services/cro-audit` ("Have your checkout audited with evidence, not opinions"). Secondary: `/services/shopify-development` for fixes within checkout extensibility.

**10. Recommendation: UPDATE `/blogs/why-customers-abandon-checkout` to own the "checkout audit" intent. This refines your call.**

Reasons:
- (a) It already has the Cause → Evidence table, which is the seed of the evidence column.
- (b) It is in the CRO category and has room to grow from 970 to about 2,000 words.
- (c) "Why do they abandon" plus "how to audit for it" is one diagnostic intent. Turning the design guide `ecommerce-checkout-ux` into an audit would blur its "how to design" intent and push it past 2,500 words.
- (d) `shopify-checkout-audit` is platform-specific and thin, and its slug would mislead non-Shopify readers.

Linking:

## Topic 80: How to analyse CRO experiments and decide what to test next

**1. Founder question and business pain.** "The test finished. Is the result real, what does it mean, and what do we test next?" Teams ship false winners, discard useful losses, and go fishing in segments after the fact.

**2. Intent and buyer stage.** Informational or how-to, for in-house growth or CRO leads who are already testing.

**3. Queries.** Primary: "how to analyze A/B test results". Supporting: "inconclusive A/B test what next", "sample ratio mismatch", "Twyman's law", "A/B test segmentation", "what to test after a winning test".
